How ShareLab works

  1. List - a verified owner lists a resource with opening hours, required qualifications, usage rules and fair-use limits.
  2. Discover - users search in plain words or filter by time; each card shows whether they are already eligible.
  3. Request - a dry-run shows exactly which checks pass or fail before submitting.
  4. Verify - the system checks identity, qualifications, opening hours, session length, weekly cap and clashes automatically.
  5. Decide - the owner/manager approves, rejects or waitlists, always with a reason. Owners can opt a resource into auto-approval.
  6. Check in - the user acknowledges the rules and records the condition they found.
  7. Return - final condition is recorded; staff confirm the return.
  8. Handle problems - delays, damage and no-shows raise incidents that a human reviews; users can respond and dispute.
  9. History - both parties see the full audit trail of the transaction.

Fair-allocation rules (published)

  • Hard limits: weekly hours cap per person per resource, max open bookings, max session length, max waiting-list places.
  • Priority score when requests compete: base 50, +purpose (coursework +15, research +12, project +8, personal +3), -2 per hour used on this resource in the last 30 days (max -30), +8 for first-time users, -10 per penalty point, +1 per hour waited (max +10).
  • The score is a recommendation. A manager may choose differently but must write a justification, shown to those affected and logged.
  • Waiting lists are ordered by this same score and promoted automatically to review when a slot opens.
  • No automatic penalties: delays, damage and no-shows only create incidents. Penalty points come from a human decision and can be disputed.

Privacy and security

  • Managers see name, department, purpose and credential status - not personal contact details or full ID numbers.
  • Credential documents are visible only to their owner and the staff reviewing that qualification.
  • Passwords are hashed; forms are CSRF-protected; every page enforces role-based access.
  • The activity log is hash-chained so tampering is detectable.
